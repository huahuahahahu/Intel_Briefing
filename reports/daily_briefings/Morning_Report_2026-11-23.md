### 1. [LingSante-Pulse: Unified Physiological Pretraining through Random-Feature Prediction and Cross-Modal Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27206v1)
👤 Linxiao Yang, Wenwei Wang | 📅 2026-11-20

**详情:** Clinical monitoring and wearable devices capture complementary physiological signals, but their partially overlapping modality coverage makes within-signal information and cross-modal relationships difficult to exploit jointly. We introduce LingSante-Pulse, a unified, general-purpose and flexible physiological representation framework that learns from these heterogeneous recordings. A shared, signal-conditioned encoder processes each physical channel independently and supports different numbers and combinations of signals for multimodal assessment and single-sensor inference. Pairwise cross-modal alignment, structured random-feature prediction (RFP) and masked waveform reconstruction jointly learn physiological relationships and preserve informative within-signal structure. Across 38 general physiological task configurations spanning PPG, ECG, EEG and EMG, the frozen encoder achieves the best dataset-balanced mean rank in each domain. In sleep assessment, multimodal staging on the HMC dataset and EEG-only staging on the Sleep-EDF dataset reach Macro-F1 scores of 0.7784 and 0.7941, respectively, while single-lead ECG apnea screening achieves an AUROC of 0.9183. Controlled experiments show that function prediction and waveform reconstruction provide complementary supervision for retaining temporal and rhythmic information. Representations learned under continually refreshed random supervision generalize to unseen teacher realizations, and correct auxiliary modality correspondence improves respiration-only representations. These results demonstrate that a single encoder can learn reusable physiological features across signal types, prediction targets and sensor configurations from partially overlapping recordings.

### 2. [DepthMSA: Combinatorial Training and Budget-Aware Depth Allocation for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27198v1)
👤 Pan Wang, Jie Peng | 📅 2026-11-20

**详情:** State-of-the-art multimodal sentiment analysis (MSA) models rely on large pretrained encoders, yet typically process every modality to its full depth, regardless of its computational cost or predictive contribution. This raises a fundamental question: how should limited computation be allocated across modalities to preserve predictive performance? We reveal a substantial mismatch between the computational cost of modality encoders and the predictive benefit of processing them more deeply, suggesting that different modalities require different encoding depths. However, enabling heterogeneous depths introduces a key challenge: representations extracted at different depths must still be effectively fused. To address this, we propose $\textbf{DepthMSA}$, a budget-aware framework that supports flexible modality depths within a single MSA model. Specifically, we introduce combinatorial anytime training, which supervises the fusion head across all cross-modal depth combinations using a single full-depth encoder pass. At inference, a training-free, cost-aware allocator selects the configuration with the lowest validation-calibrated error under a sample-specific FLOPs budget. On CMU-MOSEI, DepthMSA maintains accuracy within one percentage point of the full-depth model while reducing measured FLOPs by 45\%, or by 82\% with a separately trained skip-enabled variant. When a CLIP visual encoder dominates computation, the allocation shifts toward reducing visual depth, achieving 56\% fewer FLOPs at unchanged accuracy. Compared with ADMN, an adaptive layer-allocation method that trains a controller for each budget, the skip-enabled variant reaches its accuracy with 3.2$\times$ fewer FLOPs and lower latency, without any per-budget training. Our project is available at https://pwang322.github.io/DepthMSA/.

### 3. [EDAGoat: An EDA-Aware Agent for Project-Level GPU Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27196v1)
👤 Chengxi Li, Liang Xiao | 📅 2026-11-20

**详情:** GPU acceleration has transformed electronic design automation (EDA), yet further improving expert-engineered GPU tools still demands substantial manual effort. Automating this work calls for coding agents that can trace bottlenecks across host and device code while preserving correctness and design quality. We introduce EDAGoat, to our knowledge the first EDA-aware GPU optimization agent for this task. Its optimization enhancement (OE) mode guides edits with four cross-project GPU optimization patterns. Its mandatory validation (MV) mode requires the agent to test the edited program against the EDA constraints its changes could affect. Our benchmark covers five GPU EDA projects across four task families and 55 circuit inputs, measuring complete-solve runtime and verifying the resulting designs. Each configuration runs three independent searches per project. A search qualifies only if its solver passes correctness and design-quality checks on all public and hidden inputs. With DeepSeek, generic prompting qualifies in 7 of 15 searches and OE in 6 of 15, despite a higher best speedup on four projects. Adding MV raises this to 13 of 15 and, on every project, exceeds both generic baselines in mean and best speedup and OE in mean speedup; the geometric mean of per-project mean speedups rises from 1.11x (1.36x with OE) to 1.53x. With GPT-6 Astra, EDAGoat qualifies in all 15 searches and improves both speedup metrics over generic prompting on every project, lifting the same geometric mean from 1.26x to 1.57x. These results show that agents can use EDA knowledge to find further speedups in expert-tuned tools while preserving design correctness and quality, establishing EDA-aware GPU optimization as a new direction for agent research.

### 4. [A Frugal AI Framework for India: Responsible and Right-sized AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27189v1)
👤 Balaraman Ravindran, K. S. Venkataraghavan | 📅 2026-11-20

**详情:** India's AI debate often swings between two insufficient options: replicating the compute-intensive, capital-heavy foundation-model programmes of the United States or China, or remaining a downstream adopter of foreign AI with localised interfaces and safeguards. Neither serves a country that must deliver AI to a very large, diverse population across uneven infrastructure and within constrained budgets. This white paper from the Centre for Responsible AI (CeRAI), IIT Madras, proposes a third path: Responsible Frugal AI, which applies principle-based frugal engineering alongside Responsible AI. Responsible Frugal AI is right-sized AI: it fixes the functional requirement first, asks whether AI is needed at all, and then chooses the smallest sufficient model, meeting the requirement with minimal resources while keeping a verified margin, robustness and responsible governance. The framework applies frugality across four dimensions (data, compute, model and operations) through seven levers spanning the AI lifecycle, each with practices and measures. It calls for public AI rails, vernacular-first design, measured resource use, human oversight for high-stakes decisions, and a cap on total resource consumption so that efficiency gains are not cancelled out by rising usage. We argue that India is resource-imbalanced rather than resource-poor, and that frugal principles offer the most viable route to population-scale deployment, fit naturally with India's digital public infrastructure, and make sovereignty operational by preserving India's capacity to specify, deploy, govern, inspect and adapt its AI systems. A seven-point adoption checklist and eleven policy statements translate the framework into practice, positioning AI in India as a responsible, frugal public-value system that maximises social value per rupee and per joule.
