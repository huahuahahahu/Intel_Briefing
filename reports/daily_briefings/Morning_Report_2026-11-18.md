### 1. [Ambient LLMs: Data-Efficient Supervised Fine-tuning via Temperature Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23229v1)
👤 Giannis Daras, Marios Papachristou | 📅 2026-11-17

**详情:** Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality post-training datasets for autoregressive language models is challenging. Consequently, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is typically applied to datasets significantly smaller than those used for pre-training, which leads to overfitting and the catastrophic forgetting of the tails of the data distribution. To prevent this collapse, we introduce Ambient SFT, a principled framework for leveraging imperfect predictions to augment the fine-tuning set. In our method, a "frozen" version of the pre-trained model serves as a noisy teacher that the fine-tuned model must match at a coarse level. In particular, we require that the high-temperature versions of the conditional distributions produced by the post-training model should match the high-temperature versions of the pre-training ones. The temperature hyperparameter controls the level of trust in the pre-trained model, with the limit $T\to \infty$ reverting back to regular SFT and $T=1$ to knowledge distillation. We demonstrate that our approach outperforms standard SFT across domain adaptation, instruction-tuning, and reasoning, leading for up to $21\%$ average relative improvement to standard SFT.

### 3. [Do LLM Agents Still Need to Be Told How to Self-Improve?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23219v1)
👤 Shobhita Sundaram, Cansu Sancaktar | 📅 2026-11-17

**详情:** LLM agents increasingly drive their own learning. Self-improvement methods delegate various components of model optimization to the models themselves. As models become more capable, this motivates a basic question: do these increasingly capable models still need us to specify how they should self-improve? In this paper we study an autonomous paradigm in which, given basic primitives, resources, and a general goal for self-improvement, agents construct their own self-learning algorithm for context-based optimization. Using the setting of gameplay as a testbed, a frozen LLM agent is given a compute budget and tools for collecting experience and updating text documents, and allowed to freely experiment without an externally imposed loop or access to real-world data. We find that sufficiently capable models do self-improve under these conditions, demonstrating strategies that align with classic cognitive frameworks for self-regulated learning and constructs from machine learning literature on self-generated curricula. Furthermore, we study the extent to which the learned text artifacts transfer to external benchmarks, observing improvement across different game horizons, mechanics, and 3D navigation.

### 4. [Higher-Scoring Coding Models Did Not Get More Work Done](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23216v1)
👤 Yining Hua | 📅 2026-11-17

**详情:** New coding models advertise themselves on score improvements of coding benchmarks. Yet no study has tested if a model that scores higher on these benchmarks gets more real work done. On June 12, 2026, a U.S. export control directive banned access to Claude Fable 5 for 19 days, which forced users to switch to other models. During this time, most former Fable 5 users switched to Opus 4.8 (11 points lower on SWE-Bench Pro compared to Fable 5). We treat this suspension as a natural experiment, where the people and projects stayed the same and only the model changed, so we could isolate the effect of Fable 5 on user productivity. We tracked GitHub pull requests of 297 Fable 5 users before, during, and after the suspension. We used 8,908 developers who stayed on Opus 4.8 all the time as the control group. We also repeated the comparison across five model upgrades before and after the suspension in 2026 (1,208 developers, 15,442 pull requests). Fitting a difference-in-differences model with developer and date fixed effects, we found that having access to Fable 5 increased the 7-day merge rate of pull requests by only 1.4 percentage points [-1.4; +4.3], which was not statistically significant. Similarly, no significant increases of merge rates were found with the upgrades (weighted mean: -0.9 percentage points [-1.9; +0.2]); all five estimates were negative (95% intervals included 0, did not differ from each other). We also did not observe a significant increase in the number of pull requests merged per week (+2.4% [-4.9%; +10.2%]).

### 5. [Reading Scene States: Uncovering Safety Failures in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23206v1)
👤 Tongxi Wu, Yuyang Cheng | 📅 2026-11-17

**详情:** Vision-language models (VLMs) can provide harmful assistance by interpreting visual events, even without an explicit request for the underlying harmful objective. We identify this \textbf{scene-state safety vulnerability} and introduce \textbf{SceneSafetyBench}, comprising 375 image--question pairs spanning Initial, Medium, and Ultimate scene states and five visual presentations. Across eight VLMs, scene-state inputs elicit harmful assistance with attack success rates (ASRs) of 45.6--72.3\%. Comparative analysis across three VLMs shows that scene-state inputs most closely resemble QR-related images in their layer-wise safety-related signal profiles, while exhibiting lower average late-layer readouts and distinct signal dynamics during generation. To turn this vulnerability into a systematic attack, we propose \textbf{S3TA}, which translates harmful objectives into scene states, queries image--question pairs independently, and assembles the responses. On seven MM-SafetyBench categories across eight open-weight and commercial VLMs, S3TA achieves a model-average ASR of \textbf{71.2\%, exceeding the strongest baseline by 6.1 percentage points}. Text-side filters and visual moderation provide limited protection. These findings highlight the need to safeguard the assistance models infer through scene-grounded reasoning, beyond screening input content.
