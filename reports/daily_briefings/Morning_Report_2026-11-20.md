### 1. [Jeveration: System One Models Now Generate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24738)
👤 Yuhe Liu, Qi Li | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** System One models such as Jev return decisions through fast, parallel prediction. In this paper, we introduce Jeveration, showing that the same decision paradigm can also generate high-quality images in a single forward pass. Jeveration builds on a frozen vision-language model, repurposing its visual-semantic knowledge for holistic image generation. The key challenge is to maintain global consistency. A single prompt admits many valid images, yet parallel predictions must converge on a coherent whole. We therefore condition all positions on a shared random state to coordinate parallel predictions, so that each draw yields a different but coherent image. Since the model tends to ignore this state under direct image supervision, we first learn a distribution over discrete image codes to guide single-pass generation. Beyond text-to-image generation, we further unify image-to-image generation and dense prediction within the same architecture, so that one checkpoint supports all three tasks, each in one forward pass. Experiments show that Jeveration generates a 512x512 image in 78 ms, up to 122x faster than existing unified models, while maintaining competitive image quality and dense prediction performance.

### 2. [Game2Player: Scaling Gameplay Experience for Visual Agents through Environment-Player Co-Development](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24154)
👤 Xirui Jiang, Yuxi Chen | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** Games are a rich testbed for visual agents, demanding perception, long-horizon planning, and precise control. However, collecting human gameplay is costly, and learning from gameplay videos requires inferring missing actions. Existing datasets also cover a limited range of games, often without explicit tasks or verifiable outcomes. To address these gaps, we introduce Game2Player, a framework that co-develops game environments and players to collect diverse and verifiable gameplay experience at scale. In the Game2Player loop, a Builder agent adapts existing web games, exposes their internal state, and defines tasks with state-based progress measures and success checks. A Coding Player and a VLM Player then attempt the tasks and report problems to the Builder for repair, so that play serves as both environment validation and training data collection. With Game2Player, we build 568 verified web games with 3,436 tasks, collect 15.7K trajectories (307 hours of gameplay), and hold out 50 games as the benchmark PlayerBench. To evaluate the collected experience as training data, we train VLM agents with progress-weighted SFT, assigning greater weight to steps that advance task progress. Game2Player-9B improves progress from 14.3% to 31.2% on PlayerBench and from 30.0% to 53.1% on the external GameWorld benchmark. More broadly, our work suggests a path for visual agents to scale their gameplay experience by building, verifying, and learning from existing game worlds.

### 3. [CurvDelta: Low-Curvature Delta Modeling For Surgical Learning and Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24654)
👤 Zeguan Xiao, Zhuocheng Liang | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** A central challenge in adapting large language models (LLMs) is capability preservation: an adaptation can achieve its target while degrading general capabilities. We introduce CurvDelta, a scalable framework that incorporates the preservation constraint into the parameterization of the model update. By analyzing the local geometry of the preservation constraint around the pre-adaptation model, CurvDelta constructs a low-curvature space in which parameter updates leave the preservation loss nearly invariant. We make its construction practical for LLMs through Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (K-FAC) and a factorized projection. We also introduce CurvLoRA, a low-rank variant within the same preservation geometry, designed for more efficient adaptation. Both methods apply to preservation-constrained adaptation tasks such as capability learning and selective unlearning. We evaluate our methods on mathematical reasoning, code generation, and knowledge unlearning. For mathematical reasoning and code generation, our approaches achieve competitive downstream performance while better preserving general capabilities, offering a favorable acquisition--retention trade-off relative to the baselines. For knowledge unlearning, our main results on TOFU and MUSE show a better balance between targeted forgetting and knowledge retention than the baselines, with retention performance close to the from-scratch retraining reference.

### 4. [HINT-WAM: Hindsight-Informed Training for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24789)
👤 Phuong Tuan Dat, Qi Li | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** World action models (WAMs) have shown promising generalization and data efficiency in robot manipulation by coupling action generation with predictions of future visual dynamics. Translating these capabilities into reliable task execution, however, remains a challenge for existing post-training methods. Offline supervised fine-tuning provides dense supervision on expert trajectories, but its flow-matching action expert is trained only on noised demonstration actions and never on the intermediate states produced by its own sampler; task-specific adaptation can also compromise pretrained capabilities. On-policy reinforcement learning trains on the policy's own behavior, yet sparse task rewards make exploration and credit assignment difficult, while dense reward shaping still provides scalar evaluations rather than direct action targets. We introduce HINT-WAM, the first on-policy self-distillation framework that supervises the action expert on its own sampling trajectories using hindsight, the future video of the demonstration, as privileged information available to the WAM itself. For each demonstration observation, the student generates its action through its own denoising trajectory, and at the states along this trajectory the same model acts as a teacher conditioned additionally on this hindsight, providing a conditional action distribution as a dense training target, while the student sees only deployment-time inputs and needs no environmental rewards or simulator interaction. To transfer this supervision to a flow-matching action expert, HINT-WAM distills the teacher into the student with an energy score, a distribution-level objective that matches the teacher's action distribution from the student's own samples rather than regressing its velocity field. The project web is available in https://tuandattt.github.io/hintwam/.

### 5. [LightTAR: Light and Fast Target-Aware Representations for Multimodal Tabular Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17600)
👤 Eilam Shapira, Alan Arazi | 📅 2026-11-12

**详情:** Multimodal tabular learning involves classification and regression on tables combining structured numerical and categorical features with unstructured text and images. These unstructured fields are commonly represented by pretrained encoder embeddings reduced with principal component analysis (PCA). This reduction prioritizes input variance but may overlook information relevant to predicting the target. We introduce LightTAR, a simple, inexpensive method that adapts dimensionality reduction to the prediction task. LightTAR augments the standard PCA representation with a few target-aware features extracted jointly from the final and an intermediate encoder layer using regularized partial least squares (PLS). We evaluate LightTAR with 21 tabular models, including tree ensembles, neural networks, and tabular foundation models. LightTAR improves average predictive performance for all models and, averaged across models, improves prediction on almost every MulTaBench dataset. These gains come with a small increase in total runtime relative to PCA representations. LightTAR integrates directly into existing encoding pipelines, improving tabular learners' predictive performance without modifying the encoder or the learner.
