### 1. [PlayCraft: Designing Engaging Games from Agent Play Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26624v1)
👤 Weihao Cheng | 📅 2026-11-19

**详情:** Recent AI agents can generate playable games, but these games are not necessarily engaging. Mastery is a key source of engagement, yet designing for it requires extensive human play and tuning. We propose PlayCraft, an automated framework that uses a vision-based agent to play games and draws on its detailed play experience to revise them for mastery. Starting from an initial game, PlayCraft alternates between playing and crafting. In the playing stage, a player agent watches the game screen, uses the controls, and pursues the game objective over repeated episodes while retaining memory. After each episode, it reports its progress, outcomes, decisions, and reflections. In the crafting stage, a crafter agent uses these reports to revise the game. It estimates player abilities, predicts performance at different proficiency levels, and compares those predictions with a target learning progression. It diagnoses the game and addresses the gaps by giving players meaningful opportunities to improve through better decisions and execution. We evaluate games in fresh runs with player agents across three intelligence settings, measuring learning gain and learning slope. Compared with the initial games, PlayCraft yields larger learning gains and steeper learning slopes in most games. It also outperforms the baselines on the macro-average of each metric at every intelligence setting.

### 2. [One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617v1)
👤 Yuxin Jiang, Wenqiang Sun | 📅 2026-11-19

**详情:** Videos reveal how the world changes, but rarely the actions that cause those changes. Latent action learning recovers such supervision from visual transitions, yet often learns the underlying dynamics largely from scratch. We ask whether a pretrained video generator can instead provide the prior for both inverse and forward dynamics. We introduce O$^{2}$LaF, built on a simple principle: one prior, two dynamics. We initialize both models from the same pretrained video DiT and adapt them to their distinct roles: a feed-forward transition encoder with learnable action queries for inverse dynamics, and a causal, action-conditioned video generator for forward dynamics. By controlling information access and optimizing both dynamics end-to-end through a compact bottleneck, O$^{2}$LaF learns transferable actions from unlabeled videos. Across navigation and robotic manipulation, O$^{2}$LaF sets a new state of the art in few-shot action probing and enables stronger zero-shot action transfer across visual contexts and embodiments. Beyond generative video priors, we further extend our framework to predictive representation priors such as V-JEPA2, achieving competitive performance. Our findings suggest a broader paradigm for latent-action learning: repurposing pretrained video models to discover transferable actions and predict their consequences.

### 3. [SoK: Assessing the Evolution of LLM Capability in Software Vulnerability Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26601v1)
👤 Amine Lbath, Manan Suri | 📅 2026-11-19

**详情:** LLM-based agents are becoming increasingly capable at discovering, validating and patching vulnerabilities in real software, and vendors and researchers report that this is already changing how vulnerabilities are found and fixed. These claims, however, are scattered across papers, vendor reports and disclosures and are rarely comparable. We introduce a framework that rates these capabilities on an autonomy scale modeled on the levels of driving automation, and qualifies each claim by its correctness, its availability and the strength of its evidence. We use this framework to systematize the published evidence on LLM capability in vulnerability research. We find that agents discovered, validated, and patched vulnerabilities in real-world software without human intervention before the restricted frontier models of 2026, and that open-weight models subsequently attained comparable autonomy for discovery and validation. We then measure the consequences of evolving LLM security capabilities in a decade of public vulnerability records, advisories and exploitation data. In 2026 the number of disclosed vulnerabilities more than doubled, with an increasing share rated critical or high, and severe bugs that had survived a decade or more in widely used code were reported several times more often than before. Yet the time between the stages of a vulnerability's life, from report to fix and from disclosure to attack, has not shifted noticeably so far. We grade the mitigations proposed in the literature with an explicit rubric and draw guidance for each group of stakeholders from the best-supported ones.

### 4. [Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26598v1)
👤 Yang Meng, Zixin Ding | 📅 2026-11-19

**详情:** Acquiring every feature of an instance is often costly: a measurement can carry a monetary price, a time delay, or a privacy cost. Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) addresses this by learning a policy that decides, per instance, which features to acquire under a budget. Existing AFA methods train this policy by optimizing a self-constructed utility target rather than distilling an explicit teacher. We construct this teacher explicitly. At training time the label and the unobserved feature values are available, and together they determine the teacher in closed form: an oracle that ranks every candidate by its effect on the downstream predictor's error. OracleImitator distills this ranking into a deployable policy by soft-target imitation. Across multimodal medical diagnosis and the AFABench benchmark, OracleImitator matches or outperforms existing AFA methods across the budget range, recovering 90% of the attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost. Because the teacher is an explicit algorithmic object rather than an implicit optimization target, we can also measure directly how much of its ranking is recoverable by a policy restricted to what is visible at deployment.

### 5. [PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26596v1)
👤 Kai Lu, Chenyang Ma | 📅 2026-11-19

**详情:** Robotic vision-language-action (VLA) models can execute diverse individual manipulation skills, yet they still struggle with long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, where temporal structure, skills, and precision are tightly coupled within a monolithic model. We propose Physically Grounded Execution (PGX), a framework that offloads temporal and precision control to two classical training-free components, allowing the VLA to focus on stage-specific skill dynamics. PGX decomposes long-horizon loco-manipulation into three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine governs task progression, using physical predicates to gate phase transitions, trigger recovery, and coordinate navigation and waiting. Shared predicate semantics connect offline demonstration segmentation, online execution, and stage-wise evaluation; (ii) VLA skills operate within active manipulation phases, with flat or stage-trained models concentrating on task-relevant actions; and (iii) world-coordinate locking (WCL) maintains spatial consistency through base station keeping, object-relative positioning, and world-anchored contact targets. We demonstrate the effectiveness of PGX with extensive experiments conducted on three simulated tasks and one real-world task using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for long-horizon loco-manipulation, showing improvements in stage progression and task success rates.
