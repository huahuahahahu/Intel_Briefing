### 1. [Temporal Rank in Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24394v1)
👤 Leo Dobson, Anton Ragni | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are a natural framework for temporal modelling, yet are often dismissed due to their sequential, non-parallelisable computation and difficulty in capturing long-range dependencies, typically attributed to vanishing and exploding gradients. We identify a distinct and under-explored limitation: an information bottleneck induced by rank-constrained hidden state dynamics, which limits the number of independent input directions that can be preserved over time, as reflected in the rank of the input-to-state mapping. We show that this constraint restricts temporal capacity independently of gradient stability. To address this, we introduce structured high-dimensional state dynamics that preserve temporal separability and prevent saturation of the input-to-state Jacobian over time. On controlled sequence tasks, the resulting model exhibits rapid learning despite remaining strictly within a standard RNN formulation. Our results demonstrate that RNN performance is governed by the evolution and preservation of temporal rank, and suggest that mitigating gradient pathologies alone is insufficient without explicitly maintaining temporal capacity.

### 2. [Enhancing Active Learning Performance with Initial Data Selection Based on Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24384v1)
👤 Mohammad Mirzaee, Mehrshad Fallah Estalkhzir | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** In active object detection, selecting the initial labeled pool is essential, especially in complex datasets with expensive labeling and low resource budget. This study presents an active learning framework designed to optimize the initial training cycle of active learning for the object detection task. Our approach diverges from traditional methods by utilizing feature-based clustering from the YOLO-World model and refining images based on objectness scores rather than relying on uncertainty sampling in the initial cycle. This approach focuses on finding samples with various features and emphasizes training in areas where the foundation model is most confident. Doing so helps the model better understand different types of objects. For subsequent cycles, we employ a consistency-based sampling technique to maintain the robustness of the training process. To evaluate our method, we use two complex datasets called Aquarium and NWPU VHR-10, which are well known for their diverse object sizes and challenging conditions. In the NWPU dataset, our method increases mAP50 by an average of 6.6% in image-based evaluation. Moreover, the image refinement method shows an improvement of approximately 11% in object-based evaluation. The results show improvements in model performance and labeling time, demonstrating the advantages of using foundation models in early cycles of active learning. An implementation of the algorithm is available at https://github.com/Mhrshd-fllh/ALBoost-FM

### 3. [MoE-Context: Show the Model Only What It Needs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24379v1)
👤 Vedant Navale | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** AI agents read their whole history at every step. People do not work like that: we keep what the moment needs and hold the rest as a rough note we can recall. MoE-Context applies this to language models. The context is split into blocks. Before each step a fast yes or no pass decides which blocks the step needs; the rest stay as one-line notes that can be brought back, and nothing is deleted. In the version we build, one model makes this decision and then answers, over a single key-value cache where hidden blocks are masked rather than removed. On a laptop with Qwen3-1.7B and a synthetic lookup task, showing only the needed block raises accuracy from 93 to 119 of 120, and a masked block is as hidden as a deleted one. The model's own picking is the weak part. A supervised linear probe written into spare output rows lifts its accuracy from 80 to 110 of 120, but simple keyword search picks perfectly on this task, and the probe does not transfer to a second task. We report these results openly, along with two cheap usage patterns that block switching enables: undoing a failed attempt and running subagents without a new context.

### 4. [LLM-Assisted Multi-Fidelity Bayesian Optimization: Fusing Expert Knowledge with Numerical Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24377v1)
👤 Dongjun Han, Mingyu Lee | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** Multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization (MFBO) reduces engineering design cost by combining inexpensive low-fidelity (LF) models with a few accurate high-fidelity (HF) evaluations and sequentially selecting designs to evaluate. However, when HF data are scarce, the LF-HF discrepancy is difficult to learn from numerical data alone, especially when physics omitted in the LF model makes it design-dependent. Yet even without sufficient data, domain experts often know which physics the LF model omits and under which conditions its effect grows. Existing multi-fidelity (MF) models cannot use such qualitative knowledge directly, but large language models (LLMs) can interpret it and compare designs accordingly. The proposed LLM-Assisted MFBO adds a knowledge basis to the conventional MF model: from an expert description of the omitted physics, an LLM judges which of two designs requires the larger signed residual correction, and a pairwise Gaussian process converts these judgments into the basis. HF data calibrate its contribution and sign, so the knowledge is not trusted blindly. On a lithium-ion battery design problem, the proposed method reduced the regret area under the curve of conventional MFBO by 61%. Knowledge quality, rather than the choice of LLM, determined whether the search was accelerated, as shown by a controlled study that varied the two independently. A knowledge-level ablation further quantified the contribution of each knowledge statement. These results show that LLM-interpreted expert knowledge can complement numerical data as an information source in MFBO.

### 5. [PIPETTE: Prompt Injection Purple-Teaming for Robust LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24362v1)
👤 Lipeng He, Yihan Wang | 📅 2026-11-18

**详情:** Large language model (LLM) agents routinely interact with untrusted external data, exposing them to indirect prompt injection (IPI) attacks that hijack agents into executing attacker-chosen tasks. To uncover these vulnerabilities at scale, automated red-teaming methods often leverage reinforcement learning (RL) to generate attacks that achieve high attack success rates (ASR). Model developers use the attacks discovered during internal red-teaming to harden agents through adversarial training before deployment, a collaborative offensive-defensive process known in cybersecurity as purple-teaming. However, we show that achieving high ASR during red-teaming does not guarantee robustness gains from subsequent adversarial training. Specifically, we find that two state-of-the-art RL-based red-teaming methods, RL-Hammer and PISmith, suffer from mode collapse, repeatedly discovering similar attack strategies whose additional successes add no additional robustness improvements to adversarial training. To address this gap, we introduce PIPETTE, a unified prompt injection purple-teaming framework that effectively converts red-teaming discoveries into defender robustness improvements. PIPETTE employs a dictionary learning-based reward that encourages broader coverage of attack strategies during red-teaming. It also integrates an on-policy adversarial training pipeline tailored to IPI defense, enabling agents to resist attacks while completing legitimate user tasks. Extensive evaluations across agentic benchmarks (AgentDojo and ASB) demonstrate that PIPETTE produces defenders that achieve near-zero attack success rates on static attacks and can withstand strong adaptive attacks, while maintaining benign task utility close to that of the undefended baseline.
